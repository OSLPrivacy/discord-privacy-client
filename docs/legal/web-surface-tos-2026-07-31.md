# Web-surface terms evidence — 2026-07-31

This is a source record, not legal advice or a compatibility finding.  It preserves the
clause most directly relevant to automated access, bots, scripts, or interference for
each browser surface in T4.  A service’s lack of a quoted prohibition below does **not**
mean the proposed use is permitted.

## Method and limits

- Retrieval date: 2026-07-31.
- Archived sources were retrieved from the `main` branch of
  [`OpenTermsArchive/contrib-versions`](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/contrib-versions)
  through its GitHub contents API.  The blob SHA pins the exact archived text used.
- Messenger, Outlook, AOL, GMX, and Mail.com have no matching service directory in that
  archive’s recursive tree.  Those entries cite the publisher’s current terms page instead.
- “No directly relevant clause located” is a search result for the named terms page, not a
  statement that automation is allowed.  Re-check terms, incorporated policies, and the
  account’s jurisdiction before any product decision.

## T4-relevant reading

The evidence contains no blanket human-typing exception. Proton alone states a qualified exception, and it retains discretion to block or restrict activity that deviates from normal use or appears abusive. These clauses do not establish that accessibility-API interaction, an embedded webview, a borrowed browser, or a UI overlay is compliant. Any implementation decision needs separate legal review and a service-specific user risk disclosure.
